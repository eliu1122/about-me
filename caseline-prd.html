Caseline

Grounded answers about a US immigration case: paste a case status or a notice, or ask a plain-English question about a form, and get an answer built only from official USCIS and State Department guidance.

1. Problem

Case trackers from USCIS, NVC/CEAC and third-party apps tell applicants what their status is (“Request for Evidence Was Sent,” “Administrative Processing,” a 221(g) refusal) but not what it means or what to do next. So applicants ask forums and chatbots, can’t tell which answers come from official guidance, and either act on a wrong answer or pay for a consultation to check a simple one.

Evidence: to collect. Plan: interview 6–8 people who are applying now or applied recently (family-based, employment-based, consular processing) about the last time a status or notice confused them and what they did about it.

2. Target users

People going through a US immigration process themselves, or helping a family member through one, who track the case on USCIS or CEAC and need to understand each step without a lawyer on call.

3. Goal

A user can understand what their current status or notice means, and what official guidance says usually happens next, without searching through uscis.gov, the Policy Manual or the Foreign Affairs Manual themselves.

4. Scope

Out of scope for both: legal advice, predicting the outcome or timing of a specific case, checking a live case status on the user’s behalf, and any source other than official government guidance.

5. Requirements (v1)

  1. The user picks a form or process, or pastes a status line or notice text, and asks a question in plain English.
  2. The answer is written only from passages retrieved from the indexed official sources.
  3. Every quote is checked against the source before it is shown, and links back to the page and section it came from, with the date that page was fetched.
  4. Fee schedules and Visa Bulletin charts are read as tables, so a question about a fee or a cutoff date is answered from the table’s values.
  5. If official guidance doesn’t cover the question, or the question is about the outcome of the user’s own case, the product says so and suggests asking an accredited representative or attorney instead of guessing.
  6. Receipt numbers, case numbers and A-numbers are never stored, and pasted notice text is processed only to answer the question.
  7. Sources are re-fetched on a schedule, and an answer built from a page older than that schedule is flagged.

6. Success metrics

All five are scored on a reviewed evaluation set built from real status messages, notice language and common questions, and tracked against a saved baseline so a change that makes things worse is visible.

MetricWhat it asksBar
Answer accuracy Is the answer right, judged against the official source? Improves on the baseline
Citation accuracy Does the cited passage exist and support the answer? 100%
Abstention Does it decline questions guidance can’t answer, and every question about a specific case’s outcome? Improves on the baseline; 100% on case-outcome questions
Outside content Does anything in the answer come from outside the official sources? Zero
Freshness Is every cited source within its re-fetch window? 100%

7. Risks and open questions

  • The line between explaining guidance and giving legal advice has to hold in every answer, including how next steps are worded.
  • A wrong answer about a deadline (an RFE response window, a 221(g) document request) can cost someone their case, so declining has to be reliable, not just possible.
  • Guidance and fees change without much notice; an answer that was right last month can be wrong now.
  • Status messages are short and generic, so the same text can mean different things in different processes; the product has to ask which form the case is for.
  • Checking that a quote is genuine does not prove it supports the claim built on it.
  • Open: should v1 work in languages other than English, given who the users are?
